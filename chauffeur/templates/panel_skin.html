{# The wall panel's design system, for EVERY page. The home board shipped its own look and nothing else got it, so tapping DRIVES on the shelf dropped you out of a photographic display and into a flat dark web page. A skin that stops at one surface is worse than no skin — it makes the other ten look broken rather than plain. Rewriting eleven templates was not the answer. This file does what ha_theme.html has always done for Home Assistant themes — map the Tailwind greys the pages are already written in onto a small set of tokens — except the tokens here are ours and they carry light/dark with them. A page written a year ago in `bg-gray-800` becomes glass on a photograph without being touched, and keeps working exactly as before outside panel mode. Everything is scoped to `html[data-panel]` on purpose. In an ordinary browser the admin pages stay exactly as they are: the panel is where the look matters, and silently restyling every page of a working app for people sitting at a laptop is a much bigger promise than the one being made here. #} {# ── Emoji, on a device that has none. Reported from the Raspberry Pi on the wall: the emoji in the tiles do not render. Nothing in the app is wrong — Raspberry Pi OS ships no colour emoji font at all, so every 🚗 🍽️ ⭐ in the board, the shelf and the kid digests comes out as a tofu box. A phone and a laptop have one built in, which is why this only ever shows up on the panel. Two things had to be true and only one was. Tailwind's DEFAULT sans stack already ends in "Apple Color Emoji", "Segoe UI Emoji", "Noto Color Emoji" — but every page in this app overrides `fontFamily.sans` with `['Inter', 'sans-serif']`, which drops all three. So the font is requested here and the families are put back into the stack below. It is vendored now (tools/vendor_assets.py) along with everything else the app used to fetch from a CDN — that day came, and a panel on a wall should not need the internet to draw itself. The `unicode-range` slicing survived the move, so a page still pulls the two or three slices its emoji fall in rather than the whole 2 MB face. It is also RENAMED on the way in, to "Chauffeur Emoji", and that is the part not to undo. Android's own emoji font is called "Noto Color Emoji", and an `@font-face` beats a system font of the same name — ship it under Google's name and every phone in the house downloads a font it already has. Under our name, and placed LAST in the stacks below, iOS and Windows and Android all match their own earlier and never ask for it. Raspberry Pi OS, which has none of them, falls through to ours.
