🍽️ Where this family eats

Some families eat full meals with forks between activities and others never eat in the car at all. "Never" removes those gaps entirely. Left permissive on purpose — a suggestion you don't want takes a second to ignore, while a hidden one never explains itself.

🍲 What a plate looks like

Your words, not ours. Say what goes on a plate and how much of it — "1 protein, 2-3 vegetables, 1-2 starches, and something sweet sometimes". Argyle files new dishes into these, and the description is how it learns what you mean by each one. A plate is composed to the minimums and then edited freely.

⏰ Prep reminders

Soaking, thawing and marinating happen on a different day from cooking, and forgetting one silently invalidates tomorrow's plan. "The night before" is a human moment rather than an offset, which is why it is a clock time and not a number of hours.

🖼️ Dish pictures

A child who cannot read fluently yet learns what is for dinner from the picture, which is the only argument for carrying images at all. Your own photo of a dish always outranks a stock one.

🍳 The kitchen

What the kitchen can run at once. These defaults are exactly how a weeknight plate has always been timed — one pair of hands working through the dishes in turn with the oven going alongside. They start to matter when you cook for a crowd: two dishes wanting the same oven at different temperatures cannot share it however much room is left.

Our meals

Plates get built from these; how many sides is a setting, not a rule.
